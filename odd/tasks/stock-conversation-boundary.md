## Evidence already established
- SDK ai 7.0.9 and OpenAI provider 4.0.20 support the required tool loop. ToolLoopAgent is not a stronger recovery mechanism.
- An unresolved failed product/variant key globally vetoes the final stock reply even after a valid result for another key. A three-case read-only guard probe demonstrated same-key success clears; other-key success and invalid quantity do not.
- out_of_stock_signal is not proof that identity, quantity and variant validation passed.
- Custom recovery has late budget arming and can execute arguments different from SDK-recorded arguments.
- Conversation persistence keeps text and detached catalog references, not full structured tool history. This can be intentional, but ownership must be explicit and consistent.
- Prior tests changed catalog variants between turns. This does not establish stable production behavior.
- The last production rejection's precise inputs/DTO/quantity/variant mismatch remains unknown. Fixtures must be labeled representative, not production captures.

## Non-negotiable safeguards
Keep backend identity validation, authenticated fresh stock GETs, branch/sender isolation, quantity consistency, mutation authorization, RESTOCK fresh preflight, reservation/idempotency/CAS, accepted-request recovery and protection against duplicate intake. A failed or ambiguous read cannot authorize a write. Preserve truthful reporting of earlier possible side effects.

Do not silently infer a variant, clear unrelated failures as if they succeeded, or execute one argument while presenting another to the model. Do not use new natural-language keyword lists as the replacement architecture. History and catalog evidence must have clear owners and validity rules; stale stock must never become current evidence through history alone.

## Design questions to settle in S1
1. What trusted state identifies the subject of the current read, without a handwritten conversational parser?
2. How does a fresh validated result supersede a failed attempt for the purpose of answering, without granting unrelated writes?
3. Which tool history or domain snapshots must survive turns, and how are stale values excluded?
4. How does the standard SDK loop complete stock lookup and permit a separately authorized RESTOCK tool call, without silent argument substitution or custom recovery-step races?
5. What code can be deleted, what stays, and what must wait for actual production DTO evidence?
